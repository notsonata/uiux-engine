# UX Research and Intent Discovery

## Objective

Convert a vague interface request into a testable design problem without pretending that assumptions are research findings.

## Start with five questions

Determine:
1. Who is the user or user segment?
2. What job are they trying to complete?
3. What triggers the task and what happens afterward?
4. What information, confidence, or control do they need?
5. What failure would be most costly or frustrating?

If the answer is unknown, record it as an assumption or research question.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer, in order:
- observed behavior or usability sessions;
- product analytics and support data;
- direct interviews or surveys;
- domain or operational evidence;
- stakeholder knowledge;
- design assumptions.

Do not present lower-confidence evidence as higher-confidence evidence.

## Jobs and scenarios

Express needs in context:
- situation or trigger;
- desired progress;
- constraints;
- current workaround;
- success signal.

Avoid personas filled with decorative demographics that do not affect the design.

## Identify risk

Look for:
- irreversible actions;
- money or billing consequences;
- privacy/security exposure;
- loss of work;
- account or permission changes;
- time-critical decisions;
- high cognitive load;
- unfamiliar terminology.

Higher-risk tasks require stronger comprehension, confirmation, feedback, and recovery.

## Research questions

Good research questions test uncertainty, for example:
- Can first-time users distinguish draft from published state?
- What information is required before users feel comfortable approving a transaction?
- Where do users currently switch tools or copy data manually?

Avoid questions that merely ask whether users "like" a proposed design.

## Output

Produce:
- user and context;
- job to be done;
- known evidence;
- assumptions;
- highest-risk unknowns;
- design implications;
- optional research questions or lightweight test plan.

When proceeding without research, state which decisions are provisional.
