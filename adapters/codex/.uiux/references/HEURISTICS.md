# UI/UX Heuristics

Use these as review lenses, not as rigid laws.

## 1. Purpose clarity
A screen should make its purpose and primary action understandable without requiring exploration.

## 2. Match to user language
Prefer terms, order, and mental models familiar to the user over internal system terminology.

## 3. Visibility of system status
Communicate progress, completion, failure, saving, connectivity, and asynchronous work at the moment they matter.

## 4. Control and reversibility
Support cancel, back, undo, edit, confirmation, or recovery proportional to the cost of a mistake.

## 5. Consistency
Equivalent things should look and behave equivalently. Differences should communicate real differences.

## 6. Error prevention
Prevent invalid or dangerous actions before relying on error messages afterward.

## 7. Recognition over recall
Keep relevant options, context, labels, history, and guidance visible when users need them.

## 8. Progressive disclosure
Show the information required for the current decision. Defer advanced detail until it becomes relevant.

## 9. Efficiency
Frequent tasks should minimize unnecessary navigation, repeated input, and modal interruptions.

## 10. Recovery quality
Errors should state what happened, what remains safe, and what the user can do next.

## 11. Accessibility
Do not make critical information or functionality depend on color, precise pointing, hover, vision, hearing, or memory alone.

## 12. Responsive integrity
Responsive design should preserve task priority, not merely shrink the desktop layout.
